Method 2: you receive the Current camera image and sparse samples from the sparsified GBPlanner global graph, or imaginary local samples when no graph candidates are available. No previous keyviews, robot world pose, or camera world pose are supplied. The xyz of each supplied sample is expressed relative to the Current camera origin in its optical frame, not in world coordinates. Numbered markers on the image are projections of these samples; their labels correspond to the supplied sample ids and image_pixels. A projected marker does not prove that the sample is visible, unoccluded, free, or reachable.

Samples with source "unverified local" and local_<id> labels are imaginary positions around the robot, not mapped free-space samples or graph vertices. They provide spatial reference for proposing an observation target before graph samples are available. Neither their presence nor their projected markers proves visibility, clearance, or reachability.

Use the samples to understand spatial choices, but output a continuous bearing and distance; do not select a candidate id and do not snap your choice to a sample. Bearing is a three-component direction vector [x, y, z] in the Current camera optical frame: +x right in the image, +y down, +z forward out of the camera. Distance is travel distance in metres from the camera origin along that ray. Bearing will be normalized. The adapter transforms your output into a world-frame 3D waypoint using the camera pose captured with the Current image. GBPlanner remains responsible for path generation and execution.

Set bearing and distance to null for wait or finish. For move they must be non-null and distance must be within the supplied configured limits. Leave summary empty unless finishing.

For move, the resulting waypoint must be farther than min_robot_goal_distance_m from the current robot pose, so GBPlanner does not immediately count it as reached. The supplied distance_limits_m already includes a conservative allowance for the camera-to-robot offset; choose a distance strictly above its lower limit. Do not propose tiny movements inside the arrival radius. If the task is already satisfied, finish instead of proposing another waypoint.
