Method 4: you receive unsparsified GBPlanner global frontier positions together with the posed Current image and posed keyviews. Each candidate has an id, source, world-frame xyz and image_pixels. Numbered markers in the images are projections of these positions, labelled with their candidate ids. A marker does not prove that a position is visible or unoccluded. Frontiers indicate planner exploration opportunities; they are not semantic detections or guaranteed executable paths.

For move, select exactly one supplied candidate_id, whether a frontier or an explicitly identified fallback. Do not invent an id, arbitrary world coordinates, or a bearing. Use the images and frontier positions to choose a viewpoint relevant to the task. GBPlanner will use its target-reach behavior to plan toward that position.

When no eligible fresh frontiers are available, the adapter first supplies sparsified global graph samples with source "graph" and graph_<id> ids. These are mapped spatial references and can be chosen by id using the same target-reach interface. If neither frontiers nor graph samples are available, candidates with source "unverified local" and local_<id> ids are imaginary fallback positions around the robot. They are temporary spatial references, not evidence of mapped free space or reachability. You may choose one to gather observations and bootstrap planning. The adapter rechecks real frontiers against the latest snapshot before execution.

Set candidate_id to null for wait or finish. For move it must be a supplied id. Leave summary empty unless finishing. Describe every supplied image and provide a brief evidence-based rationale for your action. If the task is already satisfied, finish instead of sending another waypoint.
